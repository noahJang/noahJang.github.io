01

Research focus

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at MIT, advised by Prof. Anantha P. Chandrakasan in the Energy-Efficient Circuits and Systems Group.

I design hardware security architectures for post-quantum and privacy-preserving computation, from protocols and lattice-arithmetic datapaths to side-channel and fault countermeasures, silicon bring-up, and security evaluation.

My MIT research includes two architected and taped-out 28 nm cryptographic ASICs: a post-quantum authentication engine with an on-chip TRNG and SCA/fault tolerance, measured in silicon and demonstrated live; and a reconfigurable TFHE-CNN processor for encrypted inference with fault-injection characterization. My hardware research is complemented by graduate coursework and projects in processor microarchitecture, parallel computing, and system-level C/CUDA programming.

Research experience

Post-quantum fuzzy identity-based encryption coprocessor

MIT · Research Assistant · DARPA Spring 2026–Present

  • Designing a unified PQC-FIBE coprocessor with on-device biometric key derivation, combining post-quantum security with personalized bio-authentication and eliminating reliance on external wireless communication for identity establishment in ingestible devices.
  • Architecting a shared datapath that multiplexes ML-KEM polynomial arithmetic (NTT, Keccak) for FIBE matrix operations to minimize area and power overhead on ultra-constrained silicon.
  • Developing a lightweight edge AI pipeline using multimodal Siamese networks to extract stable “bio-fingerprints” from physiological signals as identity keys for PQC-FIBE.

Reconfigurable TFHE-CNN processor for encrypted inference

MIT · Research Assistant · ARPA-H · 28 nm ASIC Fall 2025–Present

  • Developed a reconfigurable TFHE datapath at 0.16–0.33 mJ/PBS for encrypted biometric matching and inference directly on ciphertext with no raw-data exposure, deployed between hospital servers and body area networks (BANs). [S1]
  • Characterized the encrypted-inference datapath under fault injection. Taped out May 2026; submitted to ISSCC 2027 as first author. [S1]
  • In the IP patent process through MIT Technology Licensing Office. [D2]

Post-quantum authentication engine with on-chip TRNG and SCA/fault tolerance

MIT · Research Assistant · ARPA-H · 28 nm ASIC Fall 2023–Spring 2025

  • Designed a cryptoengine achieving 9.8–36.6× PQC energy gains at 0.22 mm² and 0.86 µJ/Op , with an on-chip 8.5 fJ/bit TRNG and built-in side-channel and fault-injection countermeasures. [C1]
  • Designed a lightweight post-quantum authentication protocol reducing energy by 61–76% on ARM Cortex-M4 through co-optimized PQC and lightweight cryptography; validated with in vitro and in vivo ingestible capsule prototypes. [C2]
  • Performed chip bring-up and evaluation with Opal Kelly FPGA and custom PCB; demonstrated live silicon at the ISSCC 2025 Student Research Preview.
  • Extended version under review at IEEE JSSC as first author; in the IP patent process through MIT TLO. [S2] [D1]
  • Featured in MIT News and CNN .

Key-switching accelerator for CKKS fully homomorphic encryption

Seoul National University · Research Assistant · 28 nm ASIC Fall 2021–Summer 2023

  • Designed a key-switching accelerator with optimizations spanning algorithm, architecture, and external-memory traffic: 39% fewer external memory accesses , 11.0× faster than CPU , and 12.7× higher energy efficiency than prior works. [C4]

High-speed I/O and HBM interface research

Seoul National University · Research Assistant Fall 2021–Summer 2023

Quantized LSTM training and inference in a GPU-based framework
